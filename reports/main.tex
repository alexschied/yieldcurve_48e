\begin{abstract}
This paper examines the predictive power of the yield curve for US recessions under regime change, focusing on the Global Financial Crisis (2007-2009) and COVID-19 pandemic (2019-2021). We decompose the yield curve into Level, Slope, and Curvature factors using Principal Component Analysis and demonstrate fundamental regime dependence in their forecasting performance. Using machine learning methods including Logistic Regression, Ridge, Elastic Net, Random Forests, and Gradient Boosting, we show that yield curve factors successfully predict financially-driven recessions (0.833 balanced accuracy during GFC) but fail during exogenous shocks, where macro indicators dominate (0.857 balanced accuracy during COVID-19). We adjust for term premium effects using Kim-Wright estimates and document substantial factor loading drift during unconventional monetary policy periods. Rolling PCA analysis reveals structural instability in long-maturity yields around quantitative easing episodes. Our findings indicate that robust recession forecasting requires adaptive ensemble approaches that dynamically weight financial signals during monetary tightening cycles and real-sector indicators during external disruptions.
\end{abstract}
